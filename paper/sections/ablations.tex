\section{Control Experiments}
\label{sec:ablations}

Two controls test whether the design itself could explain the FEMNIST results.

\paragraph{Sampling control.}
Honest runs sample clients uniformly, while attacked runs sample exactly 4 designated clients per round. A gap between the two could therefore come from the sampling and not the attack. In the control, the designated clients apply scaling with $\gamma=1$, which leaves their updates unchanged, so sampling matches the attacked runs and no attack takes place. Table~\ref{tab:control} compares it with the honest runs over the same seeds. All five differences are small, from $-0.013$ to $-0.005$, and all five intervals include zero. The largest intervals, for Krum and Bulyan, span about $\pm0.08$, so a sampling effect of that size on those two rules is not excluded. The control also ran on a different platform from the honest runs, so any difference would mix the two causes. Since neither shows a clear effect, we compare attacked runs with the honest baseline directly.

\paragraph{Platform replays.}
The grid crossed from WSL2 to native Windows, so we replayed three Linux runs on Windows: honest FedAvg, scaling with Multi-Krum, and sign flip with trimmed mean, all on seed~0. Each pair had the same configuration hash, partition and participants in all 100 rounds. Numerics differed from round~1 in every pair. Table~\ref{tab:platform} shows that last-10 accuracy stayed within 0.002 while single rounds differed by up to 0.074. We therefore compare end-of-training summaries across platforms and never single rounds. These are three samples on one seed, and sign flip with trimmed mean shows the largest last-10 change.
